## 4. Post-2021 Migration Advisory & Intercensal Triggers

### 4.1 Post-Lockdown Migration Dynamics (2022–2026) and Why 2021 Data Is Skewed
* `[Checked]` The 10 August 2021 Census was taken at the trough of pandemic border closures, immediately before the largest Net Overseas Migration (NOM) surge in Australian history (2022–2025, driven overwhelmingly by Indian, Chinese, Nepali, Filipino, Sri Lankan, and Colombian permanent and temporary entrants settling in Greater Melbourne; `ABS Overseas Migration, 2022–23 & 2023–24`).
* `[Estimate]` Importantly, **post-2021 NOM did not distribute uniformly across Melbourne**:
  1. **Outer-Southeast & Western Greenfield Growth Corridors (Extreme Upward Shift: $+8\text{ to }+18\text{ pp}$ in Non-Anglo Share Since 2021):** Suburbs with active greenfield land releases and high-volume new house-and-land completions—specifically **Clyde North** (`SAL20582`), **Berwick (South-East / South-West)** (`SA2 212021294 / 212021295`), **Keysborough (South)** (`SA2 212041566`), and **Warragul / Drouin** (Baw Baw growth estates)—have absorbed massive inflows of first-home-buying South Asian and Southeast Asian families between 2022 and 2026. For **Berwick** and **Clyde North**, 2021 Census `AU` and `AQ` figures represent a **strict lower bound** (they are substantially *more* diverse and more South Asian in late 2026 than in August 2021).
  2. **University, Hospital & Transit Activity Centres (Moderate-to-High Rebound: $+4\text{ to }+8\text{ pp}$ Since 2021):** Suburbs that experienced a temporary exodus of international students and skilled temporary visa holders during the August 2021 lockdown—**Clayton South**, **Springvale**, **Glen Waverley**, **Ringwood**, **West Footscray**, and **Heidelberg** (Austin/Mercy Hospital precinct)—saw rapid demographic replenishment in 2022–2024 after borders reopened.
  3. **Topographically Constrained Hills, Bush-Edge & Established Coastal Suburbs (Static / Near-Zero Shift: $< \pm 1.5\text{ pp}$ Since 2021):** Suburbs with strict bushfire overlays (`BMO`), minimal subdivision capacity, low dwelling turnover, and high median ages—**Tecoma**, **Upwey**, **Belgrave**, **Upper Ferntree Gully**, **Sassafras**, **Olinda**, **Mount Dandenong**, **Mount Evelyn**, **Montrose**, **The Basin**, **Warrandyte**, **Eltham**, **Diamond Creek**, **Mornington**, **Macedon**, and **Riddells Creek**—receive almost zero direct overseas migration intake. Their 2021 Census figures (`AU = 3.5%–13.0%`) remain statistically valid in 2026.

### 4.2 Quantitative Prudence Triggers for Suburb Risk Classification
`[Estimate]` Relying solely on 2021 Census diversity figures is **statistically hazardous** whenever a candidate suburb satisfies **any one of the following three quantitative Prudence Triggers**:
1. **Trigger T1 (Dwelling Stock Expansion Trigger):** Cumulative residential building approvals or new dwelling completions between July 2021 and June 2026 exceed **$\ge 8.0\%$ of 2021 Census private dwelling stock**, OR ABS Estimated Resident Population (`ERP`) growth 2021–2025 exceeds **$\ge 2.5\%$ per annum** ($\ge 10.4\%$ cumulative). *Why:* New greenfield subdivisions in Melbourne's growth LGAs (Casey, Cardinia, Wyndham, Melton, Hume, Whittlesea, Baw Baw) skew $50\%\text{--}75\%$ toward non-MESC overseas-born buyers, rapidly invalidating 2021 baselines.
2. **Trigger T2 (Lockdown Student/Rental Vacancy Trigger):** In 2021, the suburb lay within $5\text{ km}$ of a major university campus or Metropolitan Activity Centre AND had $> 30\%$ rental tenure or a $> 3.0\%$ decline in enumerated 20–29-year-olds between the 2016 and 2021 Censuses (`ABS Time Series Profile T01`). *Why:* Indicates artificial 2021 lockdown student depletion that reversed in 2022–2024.
3. **Trigger T3 (Decision-Boundary Proximity Trigger):** The suburb's 2021 diversity metric sits within **$\pm 3.0\text{ percentage points}$** of a scoring inflection point or threshold AND its 2016-to-2021 5-year change in `AU` or `M3` exceeded **$\pm 2.5\text{ percentage points}$** (`ABS 2021 Time Series Profile T09/T13`). *Why:* Extrapolating a $\ge 0.5\text{ pp/year}$ trend over the 5.25 years from August 2021 to November 2026 shifts the suburb by $\ge 2.6\text{ pp}$—enough to cross the boundary.

## 6. Recommended Specification

### 6.1 Operational Mechanism: Why Diversity Must Feed `Score P` (0–10 Scale) and Never a Hard Filter
1. `[Checked]` **No Hard Filter (`SuburbScorecard!BL` Omitted):** Per the 10 November phone call (`Family conversations — before parents leave (Nov 2026).md` D-1 to D-6), diversity is a **compensatory preference**, not a veto criterion. 2. `[Checked]` **Internal Weighting of Community Score `P` (Derived from Survey v18 `C1–C4` & Phone Call `D-2`, `D-6`):**
   In Survey v18 (`6 Nov 2026`), the family's stated importance ratings (`Not needed = 0`, `Nice to have = 1`, `Important = 2`, `Essential = 4`) across `C1–C4` are:

   * **South Asian Community Presence (`C4` Many Indian Families Nearby `[1+1+1 = 3 pts]` $\approx 15\%\text{--}21\%$ of Community priority):** Reduced from `50%` to **`15%` of `Score P`**, and upgraded from raw `AQ` (`% India-born`) to **`MAX(AQ, AR)`** (`MAX(% India-born, % Indian ancestry)`) so 2nd-generation Indian families are no longer ignored.

### 6.2 Mathematical Formulation & Normalisation Anchors

Let a suburb $i$ have:
* $\text{AY}_i \in [0, \infty)$: Drive time (minutes, `10:00 weekday, top of range`) to the nearest Indian grocery store (`SuburbScorecard!AY`).
* $\text{AQ}_i, \text{AR}_i \in [0, 1]$: Share born in India (`SuburbScorecard!AQ`, SAL Table `G09`) and share reporting Indian ancestry (`SuburbScorecard!AR`, SAL Table `G08`).
* $D_i \in [0, 1]$: The suburb's **Stated Multicultural Mix Share**, defined in two implementation tiers:
  * **Tier-2 Immediate Input (Zero-Import, using existing workbook columns `AU` and `AP`):**
    $$D_i^{(\text{AU*})} = \frac{\text{AU}_i}{1 - \text{AP}_i}$$
    where $\text{AU}_i$ is `% other language at home` (`SuburbScorecard!AU`) and $\text{AP}_i$ is `% birthplace not stated` (`SuburbScorecard!AP`, correcting for non-response dilution in suburbs like Seaford and Frankston; or raw $\text{AU}_i$ if preferred).
  * **Tier-1 Target Input (Once SAL Table `G09` Non-MESC is loaded into `SuburbScorecard!BC`):**
    $$D_i^{(\text{M3})} = \frac{\text{Birthplace\_Elsewhere\_P}_i - \sum_{c \in \text{MESC}} \text{COB}_{i,c}}{\text{Tot\_P}_i - \text{Birthplace\_NS\_P}_i}$$
    where $\text{MESC} = \{\text{UK}, \text{Ireland}, \text{NZ}, \text{USA}, \text{Canada}, \text{South Africa}\}$.

Each sub-score is normalised to $[0, 10]$ via piecewise-linear clamping $\text{clamp}_{[0,10]}(x) = \max(0, \min(10, x))$:

1. **Commercial Grocery Access Sub-Score ($S_{\text{groc}, i} \in [0, 10]$, weight $w_{\text{groc}} = 0.50$):**
   $$S_{\text{groc}, i} = \text{clamp}_{[0,10]}\!\left(10 \times \frac{30 - \text{AY}_i}{25}\right)$$
   * Anchors: `10.0` at $\text{AY}_i \le 5\text{ min}$; `8.0` at $\text{AY}_i = 10\text{ min}$; `2.0` at $\text{AY}_i = 25\text{ min}$ (hard filter cap); `0.0` at $\text{AY}_i \ge 30\text{ min}$.

2. **Multicultural Mix Sub-Score ($S_{\text{div}, i} \in [0, 10]$, weight $w_{\text{div}} = 0.35$):**
   $$S_{\text{div}, i} = \text{clamp}_{[0,10]}\!\left(10 \times \frac{D_i - D_{\min}}{D_{\max} - D_{\min}}\right)$$
   * **Floor Anchor ($D_{\min} = 0.05$ on `AU` / `0.075` on `M3` $\rightarrow S_{\text{div}} = 0.0$):** Anchored to **Tecoma** (`AU = 4.5%`, `M3 = 7.7%`), which the family rejected as *"almost 100% white"* (`S_div = 0.0`). All Dandenong/Macedon Ranges villages with $D_i \le 5.0\%$ (`Tecoma 4.5%`, `Montrose 4.8%`, `Mount Evelyn 4.2%`, `Torquay 4.7%`, `Drouin 4.9%`, `Riddells Creek 3.5%`, `Macedon 3.9%`) score **`0.0 / 10`**.
   * **Acceptance Anchor (**Ferntree Gully**, `AU = 18.7%`, `AU* = 19.3%`, `M3 = 17.6%` $\rightarrow S_{\text{div}} = 5.5\text{--}5.7 / 10$):** Sits right above the midpoint of the scale, matching D-3 (*"a mixed area is enough"*).
   * **Ceiling Anchor ($D_{\max} = 0.30$ [`30.0%`] $\rightarrow S_{\text{div}} = 10.0$):** Anchored to the **`[Checked]` Greater Melbourne (`2GMEL`) metropolitan baseline** (`2GMEL PEP M3 = 29.40%`, `AU = 34.28%`, and `SuburbScorecard!AX` "Medium" threshold = `30.0%`). Suburbs reaching the metropolitan baseline (**Ringwood `31.6%`**, **West Footscray `32.0%`**, **Berwick `28.7% / 29.7% stated`**) achieve **`9.5–10.0 / 10`**, without requiring comparator-enclave levels (`50%–76%`) to earn full marks!
   * *(Optional Enclave Breadth Guard for Comparator Scoring):* If $D_i > 0.45$ and a single non-English language exceeds $25\%$ of total population (e.g., Springvale where Vietnamese = `25.2%`), cap $S_{\text{div}, i}$ at $9.0$ to distinguish mono-ethnic enclaves from multi-cultural suburbs like Endeavour Hills (`10.0`).

3. **South Asian Presence Sub-Score ($S_{\text{SA}, i} \in [0, 10]$, weight $w_{\text{SA}} = 0.15$):**
   $$S_{\text{SA}, i} = \text{clamp}_{[0,10]}\!\left(10 \times \frac{\max(\text{AQ}_i, \text{AR}_i)}{0.08}\right)$$
   * Anchors: `0.0` at $0\%$; `10.0` at $\ge 8.0\%$ (Glen Waverley benchmark `AQ = 8.0%`). Using `MAX(AQ, AR)` credits 2nd-generation Indian ancestry (`AR`).

4. **Combined Community Score `P` (`SuburbScorecard!P`):**
   $$P_i = \text{ROUND}\!\left(0.50 \cdot S_{\text{groc}, i} + 0.35 \cdot S_{\text{div}, i} + 0.15 \cdot S_{\text{SA}, i},\; 1\right)$$
