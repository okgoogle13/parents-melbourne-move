For Nish. This sits behind `Market timing — note for Mum and Dad`. It's general information, not financial advice. The final version will follow the Reserve Bank decision on Tuesday 3 Nov 2026 and Cotality's index for October (due about 2 Nov).

**Labels**
- **Checked:** read on the source page on 31 Oct 2026.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **For the Melbourne purchase, the evidence supports not rushing.** Prices are falling, homes take longer to sell, and there's more stock and weaker auctions. Rates are still rising. NAB and CommBank expect falls to continue until about mid-2027, and Westpac forecasts Melbourne −8% over 2026.
2. **The expected price gain from waiting is modest and uncertain:** about **4–5%** more fall, roughly **$60–78k on $1.5m**. That's smaller than the cost of buying the wrong house: **$82,500** stamp duty at $1.5m, plus selling and moving again. The stronger case for patience combines the market with readiness and the cost of mistakes.
3. **New finding: the case does not extend to holding on to Beachmere.** Brisbane, which includes Moreton Bay, has been falling since May. NAB expects a similar peak-to-trough fall for Brisbane as for Melbourne (about 11%), and Brisbane has further to go: about 6% more, against about 4% for Melbourne. On those forecasts, **keeping Beachmere unsold while waiting wipes out most of the gain**. Selling earlier and buying in Melbourne later comes out ahead, if they have somewhere to live in between. With the parents overseas until mid-Feb, "earlier" realistically means listing about Feb 2027, which narrows that advantage (see the timing constraint in section 4). The timing questions (Part 11) and the money conversation decide this.
4. **What would weaken the case:** a pause or cut in rates sooner than expected, Melbourne prices flattening for 2–3 months, or clearance rates back above 60%. Westpac and ANZ were more optimistic on Melbourne earlier in 2026, and earlier forecasts this year were too optimistic.

- C (buy first) is the worst on these numbers.

### Timing constraint: parents overseas mid-Nov 2026 to mid-Feb 2027 (stated by Nish, 31 Oct)

- **Highest rush risk: now to mid-Nov,** for example the 5 Northam St auction on 14 Nov, just before departure. Glen Waverley is over the $1.5m cap (workbook G7 $1,675,000).
- **Buying window:** inspections from mid-Feb, purchase about Apr–Jun 2027. That lines up with the NAB (Q2 2027) and CBA (H1 2027) trough timing, so the trip removes the "waiting is hard" cost.
- **Scenario B, adjusted:** "sell soon" realistically means listing about Feb 2027. Listing before departure is rushed, Dec–Jan is a slow selling season, and running a sale from overseas is impractical. If Brisbane keeps falling at a pace like its recent −4.9% a quarter, Beachmere could sell for several percent less by Feb. NAB's path implies about half of its remaining ~6% by then: roughly −$25–30k on $920k (Estimate). The rent gap also shrinks: they live in Beachmere until settlement (about Apr) and buy about Jun, so about 2–4 months, or $7–14k. Scenario B becomes roughly **+$18k to +$46k** (Estimate), against A's +$8k to +$78k. **The difference between A and B is now small and depends on Brisbane.** So the choice should follow from where they'd live, finance and the pension rules, not price.

## 5. Costs of a mistake and rules to know

- **Victorian stamp duty** on $1.5m for a non-first-home buyer: **$82,500** (5.5%). Source: savingsmate calculator, 2025-26 rates, verified 5 May 2026. The SRO site didn't load. Label: Reported; confirm with the SRO calculator before relying on it.
- **No cooling-off at auction (Victoria):** the cooling-off right generally doesn't apply to "a property sold at public auction, or within three clear business days before or after an auction". That's Consumer Affairs Victoria, as quoted by Property Observer (2014); the CAV page didn't load. Label: Reported; confirm on consumer.vic.gov.au.
- **Age Pension, if either parent receives it:** proceeds from selling the principal home, when they're to be used for a new home, are exempt from the assets test but deemed. The exemption lasts up to 12 months, or 24 months if certain criteria are met. Source: DSS Guide to Social Security Law 4.6.3.70. That wording is from the care-situations table, so confirm the general rule for their case with Services Australia. Label: Reported. It matters for scenario B: an exemption of up to 12–24 months fits a mid-2027 purchase.
