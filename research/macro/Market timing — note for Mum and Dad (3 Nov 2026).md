# Buying in Melbourne: why taking our time makes sense

*3 November 2026*

**In short:** keep looking and be ready, but buy only when the right house comes up at a fair price. Don't buy just because a house is available, and don't buy before your trip.

## 1. Melbourne prices are still falling

- Melbourne house prices are down **8.9% over the past year**, and **3.2% in the last three months** (to 31 October).
- Homes are taking longer to sell than at any time since 2020: **39 days on average**.
- Sellers are taking bigger discounts than at any time since 2019: **4.7% off** their first asking price, on average.
- **Fewer than half** of auctions in the big cities are selling.

When prices are falling and homes sit on the market, buyers have time and room to bargain.

## 2. Interest rates went up again today

- The Reserve Bank raised rates today to **4.85%**. That's the **fifth rise this year** and the highest in 18 years.
- The Reserve Bank expects living costs to **stay high through much of 2027**, so rate cuts may be a long way off.

Higher rates for longer usually mean house prices stay low for longer.

## 3. The banks expect more falls until about the middle of 2027

- NAB and CommBank both expect Melbourne prices to keep falling until around **mid-2027**.
- That would be about **3–4% more** than today: roughly **$45,000–$60,000 on a $1.5 million home**. Today's rate rise came after those forecasts, so the fall could be a bit bigger or last longer.
- These are forecasts, not promises. They can be wrong in either direction.

## 4. Beachmere is a different story

- Brisbane prices, which include Moreton Bay, are now **falling faster than Melbourne's**: **2% in October alone**, and **8.3%** since May.
- So **holding on to Beachmere** while we wait doesn't help us. Waiting to **buy** in Melbourne and waiting to **sell** Beachmere are two separate decisions. We should talk about both.

## 5. A wrong choice is expensive

- **Stamp duty** on a $1.5 million home in Victoria is about **$82,500**. It's money we don't get back if we buy the wrong house and move again.
- At an **auction**, there is **no cooling-off period**. Once the hammer falls, the house is ours.

## 6. We're not ready yet

- We haven't finished the survey, agreed on the areas, or inspected homes in them.
- Some of the hills areas we liked turned out to have **bushfire rules on most streets**. We only found that out last week.

## Your trip is good timing

- You're away from **mid-November to mid-February**. That's a natural pause: no need to decide anything before you go.
- While you're away, Nish can watch prices and look at areas. When you're back, we inspect homes together from **mid-February**.
- If the banks are right, prices will be near their lowest around **April to June 2027**. That's when we'd be ready to buy.
- Selling Beachmere is its own decision. We can talk about listing it around **February**, after the summer holidays.

## What would tell us it's time to buy

1. Interest rates **stop going up**, or start coming down.
2. Prices in the suburbs we like **stop falling for two or three months**.
3. **More than 6 in 10 auctions** sell again.
4. The **right house** comes up at a price that matches recent sales nearby.

*Sources: Reserve Bank of Australia (3 November 2026); Cotality Home Value Index (data to 31 October 2026); NAB, CommBank and Westpac forecasts (September–October 2026). Details are in Nish's evidence pack. This is general information, not financial advice.*
