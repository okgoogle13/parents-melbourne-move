# Market timing: evidence pack (3 Nov 2026)

For Nish. This sits behind `Market timing — note for Mum and Dad (3 Nov 2026)`. It's general information, not financial advice. It replaces the draft of 31 Oct, which is in Archive.

**Labels**
- **Checked:** read on the source page on 31 Oct or 3 Nov 2026.
- **Reported:** taken from a secondary article that summarises the primary source.
- **Estimate:** a calculation or illustration of mine, or a figure from an undated or low-quality source.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **For the Melbourne purchase, the evidence supports not rushing.** The RBA hiked again today, to 4.85%, and expects inflation to stay high through much of 2027. Melbourne house values fell another 1.0% in October. Days on market are the highest since 2020, and vendor discounts the largest since 2019. NAB and CommBank expect falls to continue until about mid-2027, and their forecasts predate today's hike.
2. **The expected price gain from waiting is modest and uncertain:** about **3–4%** more fall, roughly **$45–62k on $1.5m**. That's smaller than the cost of buying the wrong house: **$82,500** stamp duty at $1.5m, plus selling and moving again. The stronger case for patience combines the market with readiness and the cost of mistakes. The parent note is written that way.
3. **Beachmere: Brisbane is now falling faster than Melbourne.** It's down 2.0% in October, 6.2% over the quarter and 8.3% from its May 2026 peak. **Holding Beachmere unsold while waiting gives back much of the gain on the purchase, and possibly all of it.** Buying in Melbourne first and selling Beachmere later is the worst combination on every forecast here.
4. **The parents are overseas from mid-Nov 2026 to mid-Feb 2027 (Nish, 31 Oct).** That makes the next two weeks the main rush risk, for example the 5 Northam St auction on 14 Nov. Glen Waverley is over the $1.5m cap (workbook G7 $1,675,000). Their return in mid-Feb lines up with an Apr–Jun 2027 purchase near the forecast trough.
5. **What would weaken the case:** a pause or cut in rates sooner than expected, Melbourne prices flattening for 2–3 months, or clearance rates back above 60%. Bank forecasts earlier in 2026 were too optimistic (section 3).

**Remaining fall implied (Estimate; my arithmetic):**
- **Melbourne:** NAB 11% from −8.2% → about **3.1% more**. CBA 12% → about **4.1% more**. On $1.5m that's **$46–62k**.
- **Brisbane:** NAB 11% from −8.3% → about **2.9% more**. But Brisbane is falling about 2% a month right now. If it keeps that pace for three more months, it overshoots NAB's figure (to about −14%). Range **0–6%** more, or **$0–55k** on $920k.
- Today's hike, and the RBA's "elevated through much of 2027", work against the CBA and Westpac assumption of cuts in mid-2027. That's a risk of a deeper or later trough than these figures.

- Melbourne home $1.5m (the budget cap; scale down for cheaper suburbs).
- Beachmere about $920k (an undated estimate; replace with an agent's appraisal).

Reading:
- **C is the worst** under every forecast here: don't buy first.
- **A and B are close.** Which is better depends on how far Brisbane falls, so choose on practicalities: where they'd live, finance, and the pension rules.
- The swing from forecasts being wrong (about ±$45–62k) is smaller than the stamp duty lost on a wrong purchase ($82.5k). **Choosing the right house matters more than timing the market.**

## 5. Costs of a mistake and rules to know

- **Victorian stamp duty** on $1.5m for a non-first-home buyer: **$82,500** (5.5%). Source: savingsmate calculator, 2025-26 rates. Reported; the SRO site didn't load. Confirm with the SRO calculator.
- **No cooling-off at auction (Victoria):** the cooling-off right generally doesn't apply to "a property sold at public auction, or within three clear business days before or after an auction". That's Consumer Affairs Victoria, as quoted by Property Observer (2014). Reported; the CAV page didn't load. Confirm on consumer.vic.gov.au.
- **Age Pension, if either parent receives it:** proceeds from selling the home, when they're to be used for a new home, are exempt from the assets test but deemed, for up to 12 months or 24 months if criteria are met. Source: DSS Guide to Social Security Law 4.6.3.70 (care-situations table). Reported; confirm the general rule with Services Australia. It matters for scenario B.
