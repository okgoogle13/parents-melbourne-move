## What the evidence supports

**The working shortlist is Blackburn, Ringwood, Heidelberg, Eltham, Upper Ferntree Gully, Glen Waverley, Mentone, Frankston, Altona and Williamstown.** Treat this as a preliminary investigation list—not a completed ranking.

**Important limitation:** the collected evidence is substantially stronger for property prices and Census demographics than for address-specific transport, hospital journeys, supermarkets and environmental risks. Publishing a fully populated scorecard now would imply verification that has not been completed. The report below separates sourced findings from selection hypotheses and missing data.

## Shortlist evidence cards

The “test” in each card is a proposed inspection question, rather than an asserted suburb characteristic.

### Blackburn

**Evidence:** 32.4% overseas-born in the extracted ABS profile; published sale medians of \$1.60 million for houses and \$820,000 for units.[ABS profile](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/community-profiles/2021/SAL20252/download/GCP_SAL20252.xlsx)[^1_1]

**Test:** Can they find an accessible home that combines their preferred greenery with a genuinely comfortable station-and-shopping walk? **Main caution:** the house median makes this a materially different budget proposition from Ringwood or Frankston.[^1_1][^1_3][^1_14]

### Ringwood

**Evidence:** 35.7% overseas-born; council-confirmed Belgrave/Lilydale rail access and a short station–Town Square connection.[ABS profile](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/community-profiles/2021/SAL22174/download/GCP_SAL22174.xlsx)[^1_18]

**Test:** Does a particular residential pocket feel peaceful and community-oriented, rather than merely convenient? **Main caution:** the hospital bus evidence is not a complete door-to-door healthcare journey.[^1_19]

### Heidelberg

**Evidence:** 28.1% overseas-born; published sale medians of \$1.452 million for houses and \$700,000 for units, with a relatively small house-sales sample of 35.[ABS profile](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/community-profiles/2021/SAL21167/download/GCP_SAL21167.xlsx)[^1_5]

**Test:** Investigate it as the healthcare-led option, but verify the actual hospital, emergency services and walking route before awarding it a healthcare advantage. **Main caution:** apartment/unit affordability must not be mistaken for availability of single-level, low-maintenance garden housing.

### Eltham

**Evidence:** 18.5% overseas-born; rolling sale medians of \$1.273 million for houses and \$805,000 for units.[ABS profile](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/community-profiles/2021/SAL20865/download/GCP_SAL20865.xlsx)[^1_7]

## Conflicts and remaining gaps

### Property conflicts

- **Sale median versus estimated value:** OnTheHouse explicitly defines its “median value” using an automated valuation model. It is not the median of completed sales.[^1_9]
- **Blackburn:** different indexed sold pages report \$1.60 million/\$820,000 and approximately \$1.653 million/\$840,000. Use one dated extraction, rather than choosing whichever result is preferable.[^1_1][^1_24]
- **Mentone:** Aussie reports approximately \$1.497 million/\$697,000, while Woodards displays \$1.30 million/\$617,500. The captured evidence does not establish matching periods or classification rules, so neither should be declared definitively superior.[^1_12][^1_22]
- **Rent dates differ:** Blackburn’s current rental-listing page reports \$745 for houses, compared with the August snapshot’s \$700. These are different observations, not evidence that one figure can simply replace the other without checking methodology.[^1_25][^1_2]


### Transport conflicts

Older Eastern Health material lists bus routes 367 or 380 for Maroondah Hospital, whereas the current Victorian transport stop page identifies route **669**. Current transport information should take precedence over an undated or superseded hospital PDF.[^1_26][^1_27][^1_28]

### In-person checks

- Walk from an actual candidate property to groceries, pharmacy and station—carrying shopping.
- Check gradients, crossings, shade, seating, toilets and after-dark comfort.
- Inspect step-free entry, bathroom accessibility, heating/cooling and garden-maintenance burden.
- Visit on an ordinary weekday as well as a pleasant weekend.
- Test the routine hospital journey separately from the emergency-care plan.
- Ask which activities would create a weekly social routine, rather than assuming multicultural demographics guarantee belonging.

## What changes the relocation analysis?

| Consideration | What the expanded data suggests |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Preserving a multilingual environment** | Glen Waverley remains the clear baseline; Ringwood and Blackburn are stronger alternatives on this measure than the hills-oriented options. |
| **Finding an older peer population** | Every shortlisted suburb has a substantial older population; Glen Waverley, Mentone and Altona have the highest 65+ shares in this set. |
| **Avoiding misleading age assumptions** | Williamstown’s older median does not mean it has the highest concentration of residents aged 75+; its 6.9% is below several other candidates. |
| **Understanding the nature–diversity trade-off** | Eltham and Upper Ferntree Gully have notably less multilingual household profiles than Glen Waverley, Ringwood and Blackburn. That trade-off deserves explicit discussion with your parents. |

**Use these figures to guide visits—not to score individual residents or assume social compatibility.** For the final decision, the practical questions remain: *Which languages or communities matter to them? Which activities would they join? And can they build a comfortable weekly routine in the specific neighbourhood?*

### Data cautions

- **Age is not retirement status:** residents aged 65+ may still work; younger residents may be retired.
- **Home language is not English proficiency:** multilingual residents may also speak English fluently.
- **Population is not density:** a larger suburb population does not necessarily mean a busier residential street.
- **2021 is a historical snapshot:** do not interpret these figures as current headcounts.
- **Small differences are not decisive:** ABS applies confidentiality adjustments, and grouped percentages are rounded.

**Yes—these places belong in the search, but as distinct lifestyle options rather than simply “more distant Melbourne suburbs”.** The expansion should distinguish forest-and-village living, coastal living, and a regional-city compromise; the evidence below shows materially different transport, healthcare and demographic profiles across them.[^3_1][^3_2][^3_3][^3_4]

I’ve included **all eight places you named**, plus **Woodend, Macedon, Highton and Healesville**. **Newtown means Newtown in Geelong**, not the similarly named locations elsewhere.

### What this changes

**Warrandyte and Diamond Creek deserve attention before distance becomes the solution:** they offer different versions of green living while retaining metropolitan transport connections. **Woodend and Newtown/Highton are especially useful controls** against the more romantic choices—Trentham, Warburton and the coast—because they help reveal how much everyday convenience your parents are willing to exchange for scenery.[^3_17][^3_5][^3_3][^3_8][^3_7][^3_35][^3_1]

## Transport and healthcare reality

The most important distinction is **having a service nearby versus having the service your parents actually need**.


| Area/group | Verified transport position | Healthcare implication |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Kyneton / Woodend** | Both have V/Line services on the Bendigo corridor.[^3_18][^3_24] | Kyneton has 24-hour urgent care, but complex emergency/specialist requirements need separate assessment.[^3_19][^3_20] |
| **Trentham** | A comparable current public-transport itinerary was not verified. | Local campus offers community health, aged care and visiting GP services; do not treat it as a major ED.[^3_22] |
| **Torquay** | Route 54 connects Torquay with Marshall Station.[^3_4] | A local clinic should not be assumed equivalent to Geelong’s major public ED.[^3_1] |
| **Ocean Grove** | Route 55 connects through Marshall; route 56 also serves Ocean Grove.[^3_4] | University Hospital Geelong is the major public-ED reference point for further journey testing.[^3_1] |
| **Newtown / Highton** | Address-specific station and bus access still needs checking. | Geelong’s public hospital ED is a practical advantage to investigate, but no door-to-door time is claimed here.[^3_1][^3_34] |
| **Diamond Creek** | Local Hurstbridge-line station.[^3_8] | Nearest *suitable* ED and routine specialist journeys remain unverified. |
| **Warrandyte** | Route 906 provides a direct city bus option.[^3_7] | Compare hospital access independently of CBD access. |
| **Warburton / Healesville** | Current comparable door-to-door itineraries remain unverified. | Eastern Health specifically directs Healesville urgent patients to Maroondah ED.[^3_16] |

**Glen Waverley access should remain its own criterion.** A convenient train to central Melbourne does not establish convenient access to family in Glen Waverley; that distinction matters particularly for the regional alternatives.

## Suggested exploration priorities

These are **visit priorities**, not numerical rankings:


| Priority | Areas | What the visit should establish |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Green living without a regional move** | Warrandyte → Diamond Creek | Whether river, trails and village-scale amenities provide enough of the desired lifestyle |
| **Forest and country-village contrast** | Woodend → Trentham → Kyneton | Whether they prefer forest atmosphere, small-village intimacy or a more substantial town |
| **Coastal versus regional-city living** | Newtown/Highton → Torquay; Ocean Grove on a separate, unhurried visit | Whether they want the ocean outside their daily routine, or a green urban base with coastal outings |
| **Maximum scenery test** | Healesville → Warburton | Whether the scenic appeal survives an ordinary weekday, winter conditions and service-access checks |

**The key question is not simply “Which place is prettiest?”** It is: *Which ordinary Tuesday would they most enjoy—and would that routine still work if driving became less comfortable?*

# suggest rtesponses to geminis suggestions re: forms vs questionaire

**Use a questionnaire, not a form.** Gemini’s instinct is right that a structured survey is the right vehicle, but the word “form” undersells what you need: a guided, conversational questionnaire with explicit trade-offs, a hard “Essential” cap, and a spreadsheet scoring engine behind it.

## Recommended response to Gemini

> **Agreed on the questionnaire approach, with three refinements.**
>
> **1. Use a questionnaire, not a generic form.**\
> The purpose is not merely to collect answers; it is to help Mum and Dad articulate trade-offs and reveal disagreements. Keep the questionnaire conversational and sequential: warm-up, hard constraints, lifestyle meaning, forced trade-offs, future mobility, then priorities. A plain form risks producing isolated answers without exposing the underlying reasoning.
>
> **2. Use a Google Form as the interview interface, but treat Google Sheets as the questionnaire engine.**\
> The Form should show one clean question at a time and avoid exposing formulas, codes, or other people’s answers. The Sheet should handle the real logic: latest-response lookup, strictest family constraint, priority normalisation, forced-choice corrections, suburb filters, and the final ranked shortlist.
>
> **3. Prefer the 10-row multiple-choice grid for the priority section.**\
> Ten separate questions make the “no more than two Essentials” rule awkward and easy to miss. A single grid lets each person see all ten priorities at once, visually count their Essential selections, and understand the relative scale before submitting. Keep the Sheet-side validation as a hard safeguard.
>
> **4. Keep notes, but make them lightweight.**\
> Use an optional short “Anything else?” note after each section, not after every question. This preserves important context without turning the interview into a writing exercise.
>
> **5. Build the hybrid first.**\
> Upload the existing workbook, link the Form responses into a cleaned input layer, and preserve the existing scoring logic. Only rebuild the workbook if the migration breaks materially. This gives us a usable questionnaire quickly while retaining auditability.
>
> **Decision:** questionnaire-first, Google Form for capture, Google Sheets for scoring, one priority grid, section-level notes, and a hybrid build as the fastest reliable path.
